**Jig helps you build software with an AI coding agent, whether you are new to programming or
an experienced developer.** Start from an idea or improve an existing project, one manageable
task at a time.

You explain the result you want. The agent plans the work, writes the code, checks it, and keeps
notes that future sessions can use. You try the result and decide what should change.

<Info>
  **You need an AI coding app first**, such as Claude Code or Codex, with your project folder open.
  Jig adds a way of working to that app. It does not include an AI model or replace your AI account.
</Info>

## Spend tokens on the task, not the whole handbook

AI reads text in small pieces called **tokens**. Loading a project's entire handbook into every
task uses tokens even for pages the task does not need.

Jig organizes project knowledge so the agent starts with the core rules and the documents relevant
to its work. Other useful material appears in a short catalog and can be opened when needed.
For a booking change, that might mean booking rules without reading an unrelated reporting guide.

<Tip>
  **Less unnecessary reading, more room for the work.** Selecting relevant documents can save
  context space and input tokens compared with loading all project knowledge. The amount depends
  on the project and task; Jig does not measure or guarantee a token reduction.
</Tip>

[See how Jig builds the agent's context →](/concepts#how-jig-builds-context-for-a-task)

## Your first conversation

Once Jig is [set up](/install), paste this into your AI chat and replace the example with your idea:

```text
I want to build a booking app for a small yoga studio.
People should see available classes and reserve a place.
Help me decide what belongs in the first version.
Explain the choices in plain language and recommend a starting point.
```

The agent asks about your users and what they need, helps you choose a small first version, and
writes down the plan. When you agree on it, ask it to start the first task.
[Follow the complete example →](/greenfield)

<Tip>
  You can say “I don't know — explain the options and recommend one.” You bring the purpose and
  the feedback; you do not have to arrive with a technical design.
</Tip>

## What working together looks like

<Steps>
  <Step title="Describe a result">
    “Let visitors reserve a place in a class.” A concrete example gives the agent something to check.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Settle the important choices">
    The agent looks at what the change affects. Bigger or riskier work needs a design for you to
    approve before it starts building, unless you have explicitly enabled unattended runs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Try what it built">
    Ask how to open the result, what was tested, and what is still missing. Give feedback in the
    same conversation; the agent can continue the task.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  **A finished task is not automatically a live website.** Saving the work, merging it into the
  project, and publishing it are separate steps. Ask your agent what remains before people can use it.
</Note>
